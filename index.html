Clear 3 floors.

        
        
Use the paper’s clues and monsters’ moves to find every target. The last floor targets the king.

        
Capture them all to open the door behind the back row. Then tap the stairs, or Take stairs from anywhere.

        
Each floor shows a Par: the moves the certified route needed there. Clear the floor at or under par for ⭐⭐⭐, within 5 over for ⭐⭐, or else ⭐. Only the official piece and color have a par.

        
After you win or lose a try, practice with any piece and color. Only the official piece and color earn a shareable result.

      

      

        
Tap your piece for legal moves. Land on a monster to capture it.

        

          
          
          
          
          
          
        

        
MoveCapture

        
Obstacles block paths; knights jump. Pawns may open with two steps. A pawn reaching the far edge becomes a queen until the floor ends.

        
Pawns can capture en passant, and your rook can castle with a king recruit (a practice king with a rook recruit, too): see Special moves.

        
Keyboard
Arrows / WASD: select · Enter: move · Space: wait · Esc: clear · Tab: controls

      

      

        
Who moves first

        
Your daily piece is white. In practice you can play black, which changes the turn order:

        

          
White: you move, then enemies

          
Black: enemies move, then you

        

        
        
Red paths show where monsters strike next. As white, step off them to dodge. As black, strikes land before your move, so a red path on your square will hit. If enemies block the square you chose, pick another; they won’t move again first.

        
Close range

        
Ending your move right next to a monster that attacks your square, or where a knight attacks it, gets you captured at once, with no warning. That includes taking a monster that another one guards.

        
Queens, rooks and bishops give warning: step into their line from farther away and they close in one square and show their red path first. A king, a practice-only choice, can’t move into check, so squares a nearby monster attacks aren’t offered.

        
Tap Wait to skip your move and let the monsters come to you. A monster that walks into range only takes aim, so you can strike first.

Each hit costs a life, and turns a promoted queen back into a pawn. Running out of lives sends you back to floor 1 and costs a try.

      

      

        
You start with no gold. Coins and optional captures earn 1 gold each; targets pay nothing. After each of the first two floors, the stairs open a shop to buy powerups. With no shop after it, the last floor holds no coins.

        

          
Block one hit. Stacks up to 3.

          
Freeze enemies for your next 3 moves. Unused moves don’t carry over between floors.

          
Collect coins within 2 squares after each move

          
Every floor holds a recruit. On floors 1 and 2 it can be any piece; on the last floor, a rook, queen or king. A recruit moves after you for that floor and its captures count, but it can never capture the floor’s challenge piece: that’s yours to take. A king recruit can castle with your rook.

        

        
Land on pickups to activate them.

      

      

        
En passant

        
        
When a monster pawn jumps two squares past your pawn, capture it on your very next move as if it had moved only one: step diagonally into the square it skipped.

        
        
It works both ways: jump your own pawn two squares past a monster pawn and it captures you the same way, at once. This is called ‘en passant!’

        
Castling

        
        
Your rook and a king recruit (or a practice king and a rook recruit) can trade places in one move when they stand on one row with exactly two open squares between them. The king slides two squares toward the rook, and the rook hops over to land beside it. Items may lie between, monsters and walls may not.

        
Playing the rook, tap your king; playing a practice king, tap its landing square. As in chess, the king can’t castle out of, through or into close range. Castling is your recruit’s move for that turn. This is called ‘castling!’

      

      

        
Most days add one room modifier to all three floors. The paper’s bulletin and the top of the board name it. About one day in four is plain.

        
🌑 Dark

        
You see only squares within 2 of your piece. A monster winding up lights itself and its red path, so warnings still show. Monsters you’ve seen stay as faded ghosts where you last saw them. The rules don’t change, only what you can see.

        
🧊 Ice

        
Pale, glinting tiles are ice. A rook, bishop or queen, or a pawn moving straight, that ends a move on ice skids one more square the same way if that square is open and empty. Captures stop you dead. The move preview draws an arrow to where you’ll land. Monsters and recruits are sure-footed: only your piece slides.

        
⏳ Collapse

        
Every 10 moves on a floor, one more monster (never the floor’s target, never a pawn) walks in on the square in front of the door if it’s free; otherwise it waits there. At most 3 per floor, and none once the stairs are open. The top of the board counts down to the next arrival.

        
Your share line starts with the day’s modifier emoji.

      

      

        
Fork 🍴

        
        
End a move where your piece attacks two or more monsters at once and they light up gold: that’s a fork. Walls and other monsters still block lines; knights jump. Capture one of them on your very next move for +2 bonus gold.

        
Combo 🔥

        
        
Capture on moves in a row to build a combo; the counter appears beside your gold. The third capture in a row, and every one after it, pays +1 bonus gold. Waiting, a move without a capture, or a new floor ends it.

        
Each fork you win adds 🍴 and each combo capture adds 🔥 to that floor on your share line.
