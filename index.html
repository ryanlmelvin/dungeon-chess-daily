Clear 3 floors.

        
        
Use the paper’s clues and monsters’ moves to find every target. The last floor targets the king.

        
Capture them all to open the door behind the back rank. Then tap the stairs, or Take stairs from anywhere.

        
On floors 1 and 2, opening the door cracks the ceiling. It caves in after a few moves (waiting counts), fewer on harder days, and 🪨 Caving in at the top counts them down. Gold you grab now is at risk until you take the stairs. If the ceiling caves in first, you drop that gold, the coins and pickup still lying there are buried, and nothing else in that room pays.

        
If the targets left end up where your piece can never land (say, pawns stuck against walls on a bishop’s other colour), the game tells you. Choose Restart this floor there or in the menu: you keep the lives, gold and powers you arrived with.

        
Each floor shows a Par: the moves the certified route needed to finish that floor’s challenge. Your move count stops the moment you capture the last target, so gathering leftover gold and walking to the stairs costs no score. Your share card shows how far over or under par you finished each floor. Only the official piece and color have a par.

        
Every daily can be won. Before an edition goes out, a bot plays it through with the official piece and color: no powerups, no bought help, no lost lives (pawn days may spend up to four), and no ally but the recruit on floor 3. Each par is that real run’s move count, so it has been done. After you win, Watch the bot’s run to see how.

        
After you win or lose a try, practice with any piece and color. Only the official piece and color earn a shareable result.

      

      

        
Tap your piece for legal moves. Land on a monster to capture it.

        

          
          
          
          
          
          
        

        
MoveCapture

        
Obstacles block paths; knights jump. Pawns may open with two steps. A pawn reaching the last rank promotes to a queen until the floor ends.

        
Pawns can capture en passant, your rook can castle with a king recruit (a practice king with a rook recruit, too), and a bishop recruit joining your bishop hops to the other colour (the bishop pair): see Special moves.

        
Keyboard
Arrows / WASD: select · Enter: move · Space: wait · Esc: clear · Tab: controls
Then your recruit: arrows + Enter: move it · Space: keep it still

      

      

        
Who moves first

        
Your daily piece is white. In practice you can play black, which changes the turn order:

        

          
White: you move, then enemies

          
Black: enemies move, then you

        

        
        
Red paths show where monsters will capture next. As white, step off them to dodge. As black, monsters capture before your move, so a red path on your square means you’re captured. If enemies block the square you chose, pick another; they won’t move again first.

        
Close range

        
Ending your move right next to a monster that attacks your square, or where a knight attacks it, gets you captured at once, with no warning. That includes taking a monster that another one guards.

        
After a close-range capture the monsters still move, but none can capture you again that turn: a red path on your square waits. Step off it on your next move.

        
Queens, rooks and bishops give warning: step into their line from farther away and they close in one square and show their red path first. A king, a practice-only choice, can’t move into check, so squares a nearby monster attacks aren’t offered.

        
Tap Wait, just above the board’s top-right corner, to skip your move and let the monsters come to you. A monster that walks into range only takes aim, so you can capture it first.

        
Off-balance

        
        
Step off a red path and the monster still lunges, landing on the square you left. Stars circle its head: it’s off-balance until your next move ends, so it can’t capture you, even up close. Bait a lunge, sidestep, then strike!

Each capture costs a life, and turns a promoted queen back into a pawn. Running out of lives sends you back to floor 1 and costs a try.

      

      

        
You start with no gold. Coins and optional captures earn 1 gold each; targets pay nothing. After each of the first two floors, the stairs open a shop. It always sells an extra life for 10 gold (limit 1 per shop), plus three other items drawn for that shop and the same for everyone that day: a bone ward, a coin magnet, or a tool you use when you choose from buttons beside your lives. Hourglasses turn up only in rooms. With no shop after it, the last floor holds no coins.

        
Gold you earn after a floor’s door opens is at risk until you take the stairs. If the ceiling caves in first, you lose it, and a pickup still lying in that room is buried (see Capture targets).

Every floor holds a recruit. On floors 1 and 2 it can be any piece; on the last floor, a rook, queen or king. Step onto it and it joins you for that floor. After each of your moves you move it too: tap a square marked green, or tap it to keep it still. It can capture any monster, even a target, and that counts. Set Recruit control to Auto in the menu to let it move itself. In auto mode, it can’t capture targets. Once floor 1 or 2 is cleared, it moves itself and hunts for gold while you explore. A king recruit can castle with your rook, and a bishop recruit joining your bishop hops to the other colour.

        

        
Land on pickups to activate them.

      

      

        
En passant

        
        
When a monster pawn advances two squares past your pawn, capture it on your very next move as if it had moved only one: step diagonally into the square it skipped.

        
        
It works both ways: advance your own pawn two squares past a monster pawn and it captures you the same way, at once. This is called ‘en passant!’

        
Castling

        
        
Your rook and a king recruit (or a practice king and a rook recruit) can trade places in one move when they stand on the same rank with exactly two open squares between them. The king slides two squares toward the rook, and the rook crosses over the king to the square beside it. Items may lie between, monsters and walls may not.

        
Playing the rook, tap your king; playing a practice king, tap its landing square. As in chess, the king can’t castle out of, through or into check. Castling is your recruit’s move for that turn. This is called ‘castling!’

        
Bishop pair

        
        
A bishop stays on one square colour, so a bishop recruit on your colour would only reach squares you already can. When your bishop recruits a bishop, its move that turn is a hop to a side square (up, down, left or right) on the other colour; if all four are blocked, any free square of the other colour. On Manual, tap the green square it hops to: it can’t stay put. Now your two bishops cover every square. Chess players call this the ‘bishop pair!’

      

      

        
Many days add one room modifier to all three floors. The paper’s bulletin and the top of the board name it. About half the days are plain.

        
🧊 Ice

        
Pale, glinting squares are ice. A rook, bishop or queen, or a pawn moving straight, that ends a move on ice skids one more square the same way if that square is open and empty. Captures stop you dead. The move preview draws an arrow to where you’ll land. Monsters and recruits are sure-footed: only your piece slides.

        
🧱 Collapse

        
Every 10 moves on a floor, one more monster (never the floor’s target, never a pawn) walks in on the square in front of the door. If anyone is standing there, you or your recruit included, it drops in on the nearest empty square instead, so you can’t hold the door shut. It doesn’t strike as it lands: like any monster, it winds up first. At most 3 per floor, and none once the stairs are open. The top of the board counts down to the next arrival.

      

      

        
Fork 🔱

        
        
End a move where your piece attacks two or more monsters at once and they light up gold: that’s a fork. Walls and other monsters still block lines; knights jump. Capture one of them on your very next move for +2 bonus gold.

        
Combo 🔥

        
        
Capture on moves in a row to build a combo; the counter appears beside your gold. The third capture in a row, and every one after it, pays +1 bonus gold. Waiting, a move without a capture, or a new floor ends it.

      

      

        
The paper

        
Each story says how one floor’s target moves, never who it is, and lines up two suspects: the target’s kind and one other kind of monster that can turn up there. Watch how they move to tell which is which.

        
Unmasking

        
        
Every capture unmasks a monster: its true piece pops out. A kind of monster keeps one piece all day, so its look-alikes wear that piece as a badge.

        
Hints

        
Tap Hint beside the objective. The first is free: how the target moves. The next two each add +1 move to the floor’s score, and the button then reads Hint · +1:

        
Identify the target rings the targets on the board and names their kind. Show danger map hatches in black and white every square where ending your move gets you captured at once.

        
Paid hints count like moves: in your move count, against par and on your share card, even after Restart this floor. Flip back through the hints you have with ‹ › or a swipe; that’s free.
