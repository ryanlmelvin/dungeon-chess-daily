# Dungeon Chess Daily

[Play Dungeon Chess Daily](https://ryanlmelvin.github.io/dungeon-chess-daily/) · [Source](https://github.com/ryanlmelvin/dungeon-chess-daily)

A short daily dungeon played with chess moves. A twirling pixel newspaper hints at three disguised targets through tabloid headlines and suspect lineups. Take one chess piece through three crowded rooms, unmask your targets, collect powerups, and share a spoiler-free emoji result. Designed for a complete daily run in five minutes or less.

By [Ryan L. Melvin](https://ryanlmelvin.com).

## Play

- Your daily piece is selected from pawn, knight, bishop, rook, and queen with equal probability. The king is never the daily piece: it can't step next to the monster king and a recruit on Auto can't capture it, so the last floor could be unwinnable. You can still choose a king for practice, with a warning. It is always white, so you move first; black is a practice choice. It moves and captures like that chess piece. Tap your piece to see legal destinations, then tap a destination to move.
- Capture a monster by landing on its square. A pawn advances into empty squares and captures diagonally; reaching the far rank promotes it to a queen, and it wears a small gold crown for as long as it survives. En passant works both ways: a monster pawn that double-steps past your pawn can be captured on your very next move as if it had moved one square, and a monster pawn captures yours the same way when you double-step past it, landing on the square your pawn skipped. Taking one shows a congratulation with an animated explainer; being taken offers a **What?** button with the same explainer.
- Each edition draws nine monster species from twenty-six original sprites, with six randomized color palettes. Each floor mixes up to three species: its targets and, depending on the day, up to four decoys. The species on one floor always wear different palettes, so no two look alike. Their chess roles stay hidden behind those appearances. The newspaper runs one story per floor: the headline says how its target moves, never who it is ("Mystery guests keep the faith on one square color"), and a lineup shows two suspects side by side, in name order: the target's species and one other species that can turn up there (a decoy already in the room when there is one, else a collapse arrival), picked from the date. A floor with no other species is an "open-and-shut case". Watch how they move to tell which is which.
- Every capture, yours or your recruit's, unmasks the monster: its piece pops out of the square (no motion under reduced-motion settings) and the log says "Moss gremlin unmasked — a rook!". A species keeps one role all day, so its look-alikes wear that piece as a small badge for the rest of the floor, even after a floor restart or a Take-back. Only captured species are unmasked.
- **Hint**, beside the objective, comes in three tiers per floor. The first is free: how the target's role moves. The other two each add one move to the floor's score, like a golf penalty stroke, and the button reads **Hint · +1** while one is left: **Identify the target** rings the targets and names their species (skipped once a target is unmasked), then **Show danger map** lays a black-and-white hatch on every square where ending your move gets you captured at once, by the monsters' true roles. Paid hints count wherever moves do (move counter, par, floor toast, bot table, share card, with no extra symbol), survive a floor restart or Take-back, and can't be bought once the floor's challenge is complete. The hint dialog opens on the newest card; flip back through the floor's earlier ones with ‹ ›, the arrow keys or a swipe, free.
- Monsters move like their chess role. Knights hover a jump away, hunting squares that strike you or cover your escape squares, and rarely land where you can take them. Rooks, bishops and queens slide onto open lines that cut off your escape squares rather than just closing in. Pawns march in chains, stepping where another pawn guards them. Any monster you threaten without a guard retreats beside a defender if it can. Habits are preferences, not perfect play, and a tie between equal steps is settled by the day's seed and the moves made on that floor, so a slow floor never changes the next one.
- Monsters get smarter through the week. **Gentle** monsters are brave and often step where you can take them. From **Fair** on, they avoid squares you or your recruit could capture, unless another monster guards the square (taking it there would get you captured at once). From **Tricky** on, a rook, bishop, queen or king winds up an attack only from a square you can't take, since lines work both ways and it would just hand itself to you. From **Tough** on, the other monsters shield the targets, stepping into your line of attack or standing guard beside them, and targets stay near their guards. On **Fiendish** days a target never steps into your reach while it has a safe move, and guards set traps: they wind up from guarded squares, so taking one costs you. Pawns still only march forward. A monster can't skip its turn, so one with no safe move still has to step into reach: waiting can squeeze it. The king floor keeps its royal rules every day.
- Difficulty rises through the week: Monday is **Gentle**, Tuesday **Fair**, Wednesday **Tricky**, Thursday and Friday **Tough**, and the weekend **Fiendish**. As with the New York Times crossword, every day from Monday to Saturday is meant to play harder than the day before (Friday is the harder Tough day), and Sunday matches Saturday. The newspaper names the day's tier (for example "DIFFICULTY: TOUGH") and, beyond its clues and suspect lineups, nothing else about the dungeon. Smarter monsters are the main step from one tier to the next, and from Tricky on no floor is a freebie: the certified route needs at least three moves on each. Each day aims at its own measured difficulty, the same for every piece: generation builds the day from the floors of up to sixteen candidate layouts, picking the three whose measured difficulty adds up to the day's target, and each tier is calibrated per piece so that stronger pieces meet more guards, scattered targets, and more decoys. Every floor has at least one decoy beside its targets.
- Pillars, crates, barrels, and rocks fill each floor. They block movement and attack lanes; knights jump over them. Layouts preserve movement connectivity and pawn promotion lanes. Every edition also has a replay-verified winning route through combat within fifty moves, without powerups or companions (except the final floor's own recruit) and without lost lives, except that a pawn's route may spend up to four of its eight; pawn promotion and waiting are allowed. The route takes the stairs as soon as each floor is cleared.
- White acts first each turn. When you are white, your move resolves before the monsters respond; when you are black, the monsters act before your selected move. If they block that destination or a hit returns you to a pawn, choose again without another enemy response. A threatened capture winds up before it lands, showing its full attack path. Ending your move next to a monster that attacks your square, or on a square a knight attacks, is captured at once with no warning, including when you take a monster another one guards. After a close-range capture the monsters still take their turn, but none captures you again that turn: a strike that would land holds, still wound up, for the next turn. Queens, rooks, and bishops give warning instead: step into their line from farther away and they close in one square and wind up. As in chess, a king (a practice choice) cannot move into check, so squares a nearby monster attacks are not offered. **Wait** (the button above the board's top-right corner, or Space) skips your move so monsters come to you; one that walks into range only winds up, so you strike first. A wound-up monster whose path you step off still lunges, landing on the square you left (the last floor's king stays put rather than land in danger), and is **off-balance** until your next move ends: stars circle its head, and it can't capture at all, even at close range. Bait a lunge, sidestep, strike. Its message line has a **What?** button with an animated explainer.
- Floors one and two each ask you to capture all enemies of a particular chess role; the third always asks for the king. Every target group has the exact starting chess-army count: eight matching pawns, two matching rooks, knights, or bishops, or one queen or king. Other roles never exceed those limits. Capturing a different role earns gold but does not complete the challenge. The exit is a door in the back wall, behind the top-center square; it stays shut until every target is captured, then opens onto the stairs. Tap the stairs, or the exit button from anywhere, to leave. The square in front of the door stays an ordinary square, so a pawn can still promote there. If a floor can no longer be finished while you still have moves (every target left can only ever stand on squares your piece can never land on, counting a held Borrowed move or Take-back and promotion, and neither can your recruit under Manual recruit control (on Auto it can't take targets); for example pawns stuck against walls on a bishop's other colour), an in-game dialog says so once per stuck position and offers **Restart this floor**, the same as the menu's, which keeps the lives, gold and powers you arrived with. The check is a conservative proof (`hopelessFloor` in `src/rules.js`): it never changes play, and when in doubt it stays silent.
- On floors one and two, opening the door cracks the ceiling. It caves in after a few more moves, fewer on harder days, and a `🪨 Caving in N` chip at the top counts them down. Waits count, and **Wait** stays on screen until it caves in, so you can gamble on a monster walking into range. Gold you earn after the door opens (coins, optional captures, fork and combo bonuses) shows as at risk, as in `12 gold · 3 at risk`, until you take the stairs, which keeps it. The shop is at the stairs, so buying there spends your safe gold first. If the ceiling caves in while you're still in the room, you drop the gold at risk, the coins and powerup still lying there are buried, and the rubble carries you down the stairs to the shop, as if you'd taken them: that room is over. Certified routes take the stairs as soon as a floor is cleared, so they never meet a cave-in (`src/cave-in.js`). A Take-back can still undo the move that caved it in. The last floor has no coins and no shop, so its ceiling holds.
- Coins, optional captures, and tactic bonuses earn gold to spend in the shop between floors. Each shop always sells an extra life for 10 gold (limit 1 per shop) plus three items drawn from the date for that shop, so everyone sees the same offers: a bone ward, a coin magnet, or a tool used when you choose: **Pin** (any monster, even a decoy king, can't move or capture for three moves; floor 2 only), **Borrowed move** (move once like a knight, or like a queen if you're a knight; while you hold one, the last floor's king keeps out of its reach), or **Take-back** (undo your last move on the floor, even a lost life). Frost hourglasses are found in rooms only. Every floor holds a recruit, drawn as a mint piece inside a green ring with a +. While it waits it is not an ally: monster lines pass through it. Step onto its square to recruit it; it then joins you for that floor, blocks monster lines like any piece, and moves once after each of your moves (see **Recruit control** below). It can't capture on the turn it joins, so a monster that wandered into its lines while it waited is safe until your next move. Each floor's opening message names its waiting recruit, with a **What?** button that opens an animated explainer of how recruiting works; it never opens by itself. Floors one and two may recruit any piece, but a pawn recruit only waits where no wall stands between it and the far rank, so it can walk up and promote; the last floor always recruits a king.
- **Recruit control**, in the menu, decides who moves your recruit. It is a setting on this device, like sound, and is never part of a save or backup. **Manual** (the default): after your move or Wait shows on the board, green dots mark your recruit's legal squares (larger under a coin or powerup, so they show around it; a green ring for a monster it can capture, none on the turn it joins). Tap one to move it there, or tap the recruit, **Hold** (in Wait's place) or Space to keep it still; with the keyboard the cursor starts on the recruit, and arrows and Enter move it. Your move is already played by then, and Take-back undoes the whole turn. A recruit you steer may capture any monster, targets included (even the last floor's king), and that capture counts just like yours: the exit opens and your move count stops. **Auto**: it moves itself, capturing when it can and otherwise heading for the nearest monster, but never captures a target. Either way, once a floor's challenge is complete your recruit moves itself as on Auto for the rest of that room: there is no order step, and an order a move still carries is ignored. On floors one and two it hunts for gold while you explore, and the challenge-complete message adds "Your recruit will automatically watch your back while you explore." On the last floor, the move that takes the king goes straight to the stairs. Certified routes use Auto; since Manual can always pick the move Auto would, every daily stays winnable either way. Par assumes you steer your recruit, as the Dungeon Bot does, so on Auto it is harder to match.
- Castling: your rook and a king recruit (or, in practice, your king and a rook recruit), standing on one row with exactly two walkable, monster-free squares between them, trade places in one move: the king slides two squares toward the rook and the rook lands on the square the king crossed. Playing the rook, tap your king; playing a practice king, tap its landing square. As in chess, the king cannot castle out of, through, or into close range. Castling is also the recruit's move for that turn, and shows a congratulation with an animated explainer.
- Bishop pair: a bishop never leaves its square colour, and every recruit waits one move from your start, so a bishop recruit would share your bishop's colour and only reach squares you already can. Instead, when your bishop recruits a bishop, the recruit's move that turn is a hop onto the other colour: one of the four side squares (up, down, left or right) of its square that is walkable and holds no monster. On Manual those squares light up and you tap one (it can't hold); on Auto it takes the one whose diagonals reach the most squares. With all four blocked, every free square of the other colour lights up on Manual, and Auto takes the nearest in king steps; ties go by the day's seed. The hop never captures, and like castling it is the recruit's move for that turn. It shows a **Holy duo!** callout, and its message has a **What?** button with an animated explainer: two bishops on opposite colours cover every square, which chess players call the bishop pair. It counts as a feat but adds nothing to the share card. The rule is the position: a bishop recruit standing on your bishop's colour always hops, so it happens again only if a Borrowed knight move puts your bishop on the recruit's colour. Floors one and two only, since the last floor's recruit is always a king.
- Tactics pay. **Fork 🔱**: end a move where your piece attacks two or more monsters at once (by the real capture rules: walls and other monsters block lines, knights jump) and they light up gold with a brief **Fork!** callout. Capture one of them on your very next move for +2 bonus gold. **Combo 🔥**: capture on moves in a row; a counter appears from the second capture, and the third capture in a row, and every one after it, pays +1 bonus gold. Waiting, a move without a capture, or a new floor ends a combo. The first fork you win and your first combo show a congratulation with an animated explainer. Tactics change only gold and feedback, never how anything moves or captures.
- Complete all three 8×8 rooms with unlimited moves.
- Room modifiers: each edition draws one modifier from its date, applied to all three floors and announced in a newspaper bulletin. About half the days are plain. 🧊 **Ice**: eight floor tiles per room are ice. Your rook, bishop or queen, or your pawn moving straight, that ends a non-capturing move on ice skids one more square the same way if that square is open and empty; the move preview draws an arrow to the landing square. Monsters and recruits never slide. 🧱 **Collapse**: every 10 moves on a floor, one more monster (one of the floor's decoy species, never a pawn or the target) enters on the square in front of the door, at most three per floor and none after the stairs open. It never waits: if the player, the recruit or a monster stands there, it enters on the nearest walkable square holding none of them (in king steps from the door square, ties by the day's seed). It never strikes as it lands; like any monster that comes into range, it winds up first. It only comes as a role with room left under a chess army's counts, so the board never holds a second king or queen; when neither decoy species has room, that arrival is called off (it still counts toward the three).
- Each floor has a **par**: about how many moves a careful player needs there. The Dungeon Bot below plays each floor forty times like a person would, steering its recruit, and par is its middle result; if it can't clear a floor, par is the certified route's moves there plus a quarter, rounded up. The status line shows `Par N` from the start of the floor, never a route, and clearing the first or second floor shows a toast with your moves against par; the results paper lists every floor. Moves count as in the bot comparison: every move and wait on that floor in the current try until its challenge is complete, plus one per paid hint, including moves made before a **Restart this floor** (which restores the lives, gold and powers you arrived with, but not the move count). Losing all lives or restarting today's dungeon starts a new try with fresh counts. Practice runs with a different piece or color have no par.
- The share card is spoiler-free and golf-style. Its title gives the edition's date (with the year only for an edition from a past year) and your starting piece as a black chess symbol, whatever your color; archive and practice cards add **ARCHIVE** or **PRACTICE** after the game's name. The next line lists each floor reached, two spaces apart: the floor's marker (🟥 ⬛ 👑), its moves against par, paid hints included (`E` for even, `·` without a par), and a 🇫🇷, 🏰 or 🔱 if you took en passant, castled or won a fork on that floor. A link to the game closes the card:
  ```
  Dungeon Chess Daily · Nov 7 ♝
  🟥 +2  ⬛ E 🇫🇷  👑 −12 🔱
  https://ryanlmelvin.github.io/dungeon-chess-daily/
  ```
- Lives match the number of that piece in a chess army: eight pawns, two rooks, knights, or bishops, and one queen (or king, in practice). Each capture costs one life unless a shield absorbs it. A promoted pawn keeps its remaining lives but becomes a pawn again after losing one, returning to a safe square if needed. Losing all lives restarts the same daily dungeon from floor one, resetting gold and powers.

After you win the daily run with the official piece, the result paper shows the **Dungeon Bot**: your moves per floor and in total, beside the bot's. The bot plays like a careful person. It sees only what you see: it knows the target species, but learns another species' role only by capturing one, and it treats squares next to an unknown monster, or a knight's jump from it, as unsafe. It steers its recruit as you would on Manual, never goes out of its way for a powerup (a pawn does walk onto one standing on its own file, its only way forward), and plays each floor from its start, so its moves on each floor are that floor's par. On a day it can't clear, the paper shows the route the fairness checker found when it certified the dungeon instead (no powerups, and no lost lives except on pawn days, where it may spend up to four). **Watch the bot's run** replays the bot's run on the board at a readable pace, with pause, step, restart, and speed controls. Practice wins never show it, so it can't spoil a daily run still in progress. The in-game status line counts your moves on the current floor in the current try (restarts of the floor included), the same count its par is measured against, and stops (marked ✓) once the floor's challenge is complete; practice runs show the same floor count without a par.

Everyone gets the same UTC-dated edition. After winning, losing all lives, or running out of legal moves, choose any of the six pieces in white or black for unlimited practice. The rooms are the official challenge's, fitted to the piece you choose: coins, power-ups and the waiting recruit it couldn't reach move to squares it can, and walls that box it in at the start are cleared (a pawn plays the room as it is). Alternate pieces are not checked for solvability and may be unable to win. Only runs using the official starting piece and color offer emoji sharing, including labeled practice replays. Pawn promotion does not change sharing eligibility. Practice saves separately from the daily run and resumes after reloading; use **Return to daily challenge** in the menu to revisit official progress or results. Emoji sharing reveals your result without exposing the newspaper clues, monster movement roles, or route.

Open **Archive** in the menu to play any edition you missed. It lists every past edition by date, back to the first, with this device's result for each: won, lost, in progress, or not played. A past edition is the same certified dungeon everyone got that day and keeps its own save, practice runs included. Its shared result is labeled **ARCHIVE** so it never passes for today's. Use **Back to today** to return to the current edition.

The mobile game gives the board the screen. Open the in-game menu for the paper, instructions, sound, music, recruit control, saved progress, or a downloaded update. On a brand-new device (nothing saved yet), a three-page **Quick start** opens by itself the first time you enter a dungeon: the goal, monsters in disguise, and danger. **Play** (or ✕) starts the game; everything else the game explains as it comes up. It never opens on its own again, and devices with existing saves never see it. The seen flag stays out of backups. **How to play** in the menu is the full manual: the Quick start first, then short pages on every rule, each sized to fit a phone screen (down to an iPhone SE in Safari) without scrolling.

## Install and offline use

Use **Install** from the newspaper or result screen. The installation dialog includes instructions for Safari on iPhone/iPad, Chrome on Android, and desktop browsers. After its first complete download, the game and future deterministic daily editions work offline. Three.js, sprites, and other runtime assets are bundled locally.

Progress saves automatically on the device. **Download backup** and **Restore backup** in the menu transfer saved editions between browsers or installed apps; there is no account or automatic cloud synchronization. Before moving to an installed app, download a backup in the browser and restore it in the app. Restore merges saved days: won runs take priority, playable runs take priority over failed attempts, unfinished runs keep the later room, and ties keep the current run. Existing sound preferences stay unchanged. **Enable persistent storage** asks the browser to protect local data from automatic cleanup. The browser may grant or decline the request, and clearing browser data can still erase progress, so keep a backup. Certified editions use version 9 save keys. Earlier ruleset runs remain archived and included in backups; their old layouts are not loaded into the new certified editions.

Updates download in the background. **Update available**, including in the game menu, saves progress before reloading and refuses activation if saving fails. Other open windows keep their own reload choice. [Update recovery](https://ryanlmelvin.github.io/dungeon-chess-daily/update.html) works independently of the main app and preserves stored progress. Service-worker caches and saves are scoped to this project's path.

## Develop

Requires Node.js 22.12 or newer.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
node scripts/serve.mjs
```

The production preview serves `dist/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4320/dungeon-chess-daily/`, matching the GitHub Pages project prefix. Use `HOST` and `PORT` environment variables to change its address. Development mode does not register the production service worker.

`src/game.js` exposes the certified game API. `src/rules.js` contains the shared gameplay simulator; `src/tactics.js` scores forks and combos (bonus gold and feats only) and `src/tactics-art.js` draws their explainers; `src/generation.js` and `src/fairness.js` generate and replay-certify editions. `src/renderer.js` renders the pixelated Three.js board. `src/sprites.js` contains original monster and chess-piece pixel art. `src/main.js` owns input, the newspaper, local progress, and sharing. A move or wait may carry a recruit order (`recruit: { x, y }` or `'hold'`) for Manual recruit control; without one the recruit's own AI moves it (Auto), as on every certified route, which never carries one. `recruitChoice` in `src/rules.js` previews the recruit's legal squares for that order without changing anything (no captures on the move it joins; on the bishop pair's hop, the squares of the other colour it may hop to, flagged `hop`; on a cleared floor one or two, none: the recruit moves itself there).

The build fingerprints shipped content and generates an integrity-verified offline cache. `dist/` is generated and is not committed. Run the engine and PWA tests before publishing. Browser checks should cover a complete run, touch input, save/resume, newspaper hints, sharing, installation, offline reopening, and updating an older installed release. Physical installed-iPhone behavior requires device testing.

## Generation and fairness

Generation uses fixed structural constraints, at most sixteen deterministic candidate searches, and a replay-verified fallback bank. It never runs an open-ended repair loop or treats an unfinished search as proof. The search uses fixed work budgets, so different device speeds cannot change the daily edition. It solves one floor at a time, starting each floor where the route so far arrives; only lives carry over between floors. It judges how far a target is by real moves around the room's walls, and it takes the stairs as soon as a floor is cleared. Every stationary pickup also passes a piece-specific terrain reachability check on every floor, including bishop color restrictions and pawn promotion; knights may revisit squares. Difficulty tiers vary obstacles, decoys, guards, target placement, and measured attack-lane exposure separately from the no-powerup victory requirement, and each weekday has a target for the capped human gap, which counts the extra moves a bot that plays like a person needs beyond the certifying search's route. A day may take each of its three floors from a different candidate layout: every floor has its own seed, and the stitched route is certified as one whole run.

Every build certifies each edition from launch through two years past the build date and ships the results in `src/editions.js` (`npm run build:editions`; incremental, so it only searches new or stale dates), so opening a day replays a stored route in milliseconds instead of searching. A date past that window searches once on the device, then the result is remembered in local storage. Run `npm run verify:fallbacks` to replay all 75 tier/piece/modifier combinations (plain, ice and collapse), or `npm run audit:dungeons -- 2026-11-05 365` to audit a year of daily editions. After any change to rules, monster AI, room modifiers, search, or the human-like bot, recalibrate with `npm run calibrate:shortlist` then `npm run calibrate:confirm` (or `npm run calibrate:difficulty` for the brute force; with sixteen candidates a date and forty bot runs a floor, either takes roughly eight times as long as it did with four candidates and five runs), then rebuild with `node scripts/build-fallbacks.mjs` and `npm run build:editions -- --full`. [Generation and fairness](docs/generation.md) explains the guarantee, the capped human gap and the tiers, calibration, and versioning requirements. Par comes from a second bot that plays like a person and steers its recruit (`src/human-bot.js`, `src/recruit-policy.js`), stored with each edition. How far it falls behind the solver, counting at most ten moves per run so that one floor the bot can't play doesn't swamp a day, is the capped human gap; generation runs the bot on every candidate route it finds and assembles the day from the candidates' floors whose gaps add up closest to the day's target, the most evenly spread when several land on it. The older difficulty score, which measures how dangerous the solver's route was rather than how hard a person finds the day, is kept beside it, and `node scripts/measure-human-par.mjs` reports both over the stored editions.

## Publish

Push to `main`. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` installs dependencies, runs tests, builds, and deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages. Set the repository's Pages source to **GitHub Actions**. Asset URLs, manifest scope, and worker registration use relative paths. After deployment, verify the live `release.json` and play the published edition.
