# Dungeon Chess Daily

[Play Dungeon Chess Daily](https://dungeonchess.ryanlmelvin.com/) · [Source](https://github.com/ryanlmelvin/dungeon-chess-daily)

A short daily dungeon played with chess moves. A twirling pixel newspaper pictures three disguised targets under tabloid headlines that riddle how they move. Take one chess piece through three crowded rooms, unmask your targets, collect powerups, and share a spoiler-free emoji result. Designed for a complete daily run in five minutes or less.

By [Ryan L. Melvin](https://ryanlmelvin.com).

## Play

- Your daily piece is selected from pawn, knight, bishop, rook, and queen with equal probability. The king is never the daily piece: it can't step next to the monster king, so only its recruit could capture it, and a certified line has the recruit take a target only on a Tandem floor, never the king. You can still choose a king for practice, with a warning. It is always white, so you move first; black is a practice choice. It moves and captures like that chess piece. Tap your piece to see legal destinations, then tap a destination to move.
- Capture a monster by landing on its square. A pawn advances into empty squares and captures diagonally; reaching the far rank promotes it to a queen, and it wears a small gold crown for as long as it survives. En passant works both ways: a monster pawn that double-steps past your pawn can be captured on your very next move as if it had moved one square, and a monster pawn captures yours the same way when you double-step past it, landing on the square your pawn skipped. Your first one on a device shows a congratulation with an animated explainer; after that its line keeps a **What?** button, as being taken does, and the journal has its page.
- Each edition draws nine monster species from twenty-six original sprites, with six randomized color palettes. Each floor mixes up to three species: its targets and, depending on the day, up to four decoys. The species on one floor always wear different palettes, so no two look alike. Their chess roles stay hidden behind those appearances. The newspaper runs one story per floor: the headline says how its target moves without naming it ("Mystery guests keep the faith on one square color"), and a picture shows the target's species.
- Every capture, yours or your recruit's, unmasks the monster: its piece pops out of the square (no motion under reduced-motion settings) and the log says "Moss gremlin unmasked — a rook!". A species keeps one role all day, so its look-alikes wear that piece as a small badge for the rest of the floor, even after a floor restart or a Take-back. Only captured species are unmasked. Press and hold an unmasked monster (or rest the mouse on it, or put the keyboard cursor on it and press **I**) to see that one monster's squares, by the piece its badge shows and the board as it stands: red-and-white stripes where ending your move gets you captured by it at once, amber dots where it can move, amber rings where it can capture after a wind-up (your piece, or a monster it guards), and its own red attack path drawn bolder with an arrowhead while the other monsters' paths fade. The marks last while you hold, a couple of seconds longer after a finger lifts, and any tap or move clears them; a held press on a monster is never a tap, and a masked monster shows nothing at all.
- **Hint**, beside the objective, comes in three tiers per floor. The first is free: how the target's role moves. The other two each add one move to the floor's score, like a golf penalty stroke, and the button reads **Hint · +1** while one is left: **Identify the target** rings the targets and names their species (skipped once a target is unmasked), then **Show danger map** lays a black-and-white hatch on every square where ending your move gets you captured at once, by the monsters' true roles. Paid hints count wherever moves do (move counter, par, the shop's par line, bot table, share card, with no extra symbol), survive a floor restart or Take-back, and can't be bought once the floor's challenge is complete. The hint dialog opens on the newest card; flip back through the floor's earlier ones with ‹ ›, the arrow keys or a swipe, free.
- Monsters move like their chess role. Knights hover a jump away, hunting squares that strike you or cover your escape squares, and rarely land where you can take them. Rooks, bishops and queens slide onto open lines that cut off your escape squares rather than just closing in. Pawns march in chains, stepping where another pawn guards them. Any monster you threaten without a guard retreats beside a defender if it can. Habits are preferences, not perfect play, and a tie between equal steps is settled by the day's seed and the moves made on that floor, so a slow floor never changes the next one.
- Every daily plays the same monsters, **Fair**: they avoid squares you or your recruit could capture, unless another monster guards the square (taking it there would get you captured at once). Pawns still only march forward. A monster can't skip its turn, so one with no safe move still has to step into reach: waiting can squeeze it. The king floor keeps its royal rules. Practice can choose any of five rulesets. **Gentle** monsters are brave and often step where you can take them. From **Tricky** on, a rook, bishop, queen or king winds up an attack only from a square you can't take, since lines work both ways and it would just hand itself to you. From **Tough** on, the other monsters shield the targets, stepping into your line of attack or standing guard beside them, and targets stay near their guards. **Fiendish** targets never step into your reach while they have a safe move, and guards set traps: they wind up from guarded squares, so taking one costs you.
- Every floor is composed around a short planted line: three or four moves, waits included, built on one idea, such as baiting a lunge and sidestepping it, squeezing a target out of a walled pocket, timing a pawn's diagonal, or a mating net on the king floor. An exact search over every line of play proves that nothing wins sooner and that the line uses its idea, and a bot that plays like a person checks that the floor is no slog. Each day climbs within itself: floor 1 shows an idea plainly, floor 2 often asks for a subtler form of the same idea (a target that paces squares your piece can never land on, so the idea is the only way in), and the king floor ends in a net. Floors 1 and 2 hint at the key square: a coin or a powerup lies there, or on floor 1 a sleeping decoy stands there, looking like the floor's other sleepers. The king floor's net goes unhinted. Each floor starts you on its own square of your first two ranks. The newspaper shows the edition's number (for example "EDITION NO. 14") and, beyond its headlines and pictures of the targets, nothing else about the dungeon.
- Pillars, crates, barrels, and rocks shape each room. They block movement and attack lanes; knights jump over them. The decoys that aren't part of the idea roam close enough to press in while it plays out; on floor 1 they sleep for as many moves as the line, then wake and hunt. Every edition's planted lines are its certificate: replayed through the engine as one run, they clear all three floors without powerups or companions (except the final floor's own recruit, which moves itself there, and a Tandem floor's, on the line's orders) and without being captured, and take the stairs as soon as each floor is cleared.
- **Tandem floors.** About one day in six (a third of the days whose piece and targets allow it, drawn by the date: a knight, bishop or rook against a pair of either of the other two, or a queen against knights), floor 1 or 2 is a Tandem. Its two targets stand walled in, each guarding the other's square: take one and the other takes you at once. Your recruit can't be taken. It waits one move from your start: step onto it and post it, step to your own post while it takes one target on your order, then take the other, no longer guarded. That is three moves, so par is 4, and a coin marks your post. The journal's Tandem page is found by clearing one at par.
- White acts first each turn. When you are white, your move resolves before the monsters respond; when you are black, the monsters act before your selected move. If they block that destination or a hit returns you to a pawn, choose again without another enemy response. A threatened capture winds up before it lands, showing its full attack path. Ending your move next to a monster that attacks your square, or on a square a knight attacks, is captured at once with no warning, including when you take a monster another one guards. After a close-range capture the monsters still take their turn, but none captures you again that turn: a strike that would land holds, still wound up, for the next turn. Queens, rooks, and bishops give warning instead: step into their line from farther away and they close in one square and wind up. As in chess, a king (a practice choice) cannot move into check, so squares a nearby monster attacks are not offered. **Wait** (the button above the board's top-right corner, or Space) skips your move so monsters come to you; one that walks into range only winds up, so you strike first. A wound-up monster whose path you step off still lunges, landing on the square you left (the last floor's king stays put rather than land in danger), and is **off-balance** until your next move ends: stars circle its head, and it can't capture at all, even at close range. Bait a lunge, sidestep, strike. Its message line has a **What?** button with an animated explainer.
- Floors one and two each ask you to capture all enemies of a particular chess role: two pawns, rooks, knights or bishops, or one queen. A decoy species comes in the same number where it can, so counting copies won't name the target. The third floor always asks for the king, under two chess rules: checkmate wins (the king is attacked, has no safe square, and no guard can block), and it never takes a piece your king recruit guards. Your own king is with you on that floor from the first move: there is nothing to step onto, the floor's opening line and a stamp over the board say so, and the first time on a device an explainer does too. You move it after each of your moves, as you do any recruit. Capturing a different role earns gold but does not complete the challenge. The exit is a door in the back wall, behind the top-center square; it stays shut until every target is captured, then opens onto the stairs. Tap the stairs, or the exit button from anywhere, to leave. The square in front of the door stays an ordinary square, so a pawn can still promote there. If a floor can no longer be finished while you still have moves (every target left can only ever stand on squares your piece can never land on, counting a held Borrowed move or Take-back and promotion, and neither can your recruit; for example pawns stuck against walls on a bishop's other colour), an in-game dialog says so once per stuck position and offers **Restart this floor**, the same as the menu's, which keeps the gold and powers you arrived with and starts the floor's move count over. The check is a conservative proof (`hopelessFloor` in `src/rules.js`): it never changes play, and when in doubt it stays silent.
- On floors one and two, opening the door cracks the ceiling. It caves in after a few more moves (six against the daily's monsters; in practice, sooner against smarter ones), and a `🪨 Caving in N` chip at the top counts them down. Waits count, and **Wait** stays on screen until it caves in, so you can gamble on a monster walking into range. Gold you earn after the door opens (coins, optional captures, fork and combo bonuses) shows as at risk, as in `12 gold · 3 at risk`, until you take the stairs, which keeps it. The shop is at the stairs, so buying there spends your safe gold first. If the ceiling caves in while you're still in the room, you drop the gold at risk, the coins and powerup still lying there are buried, and the rubble carries you down the stairs to the shop, as if you'd taken them: that room is over. Certified routes take the stairs as soon as a floor is cleared, so they never meet a cave-in (`src/cave-in.js`). A Take-back can still undo the move that caved it in. The last floor has no coins and no shop, so its ceiling holds.
- Coins, optional captures, and tactic bonuses earn gold to spend in the shop between floors. Each shop sells three items drawn from the date for that shop, so everyone sees the same offers: a bone ward (blocks one capture: a pale ring circles your piece while you hold one, and when it blocks, the ring bursts, a **Bone ward broke!** stamp shows and no move is added), a coin magnet, or a tool used when you choose: **Pin** (any monster, even a decoy king, can't move or capture for three moves; floor 2 only), **Borrowed move** (move once like a knight, or like a queen if you're a knight; while you hold one, the last floor's king keeps out of its reach), or **Take-back** (undo your last move on the floor, even one that got you captured). Frost hourglasses are found in rooms only; one freezes every monster for three moves, except the last floor's king, which keeps moving while its guards are frozen. Every floor holds a recruit, drawn as a mint piece inside a green ring with a +. While it waits it is not an ally: monster lines pass through it. Step onto its square to recruit it; it then joins you for that floor, blocks monster lines like any piece, and moves once after each of your moves (see **Your recruit's orders** below). It can't capture on the turn it joins, so a monster that wandered into its lines while it waited is safe until your next move. Each floor's opening message names its waiting recruit, with a **What?** button that opens an animated explainer of how recruiting works; it never opens by itself. Floors one and two may recruit any piece, but a pawn recruit only waits where no wall stands between it and the far rank, so it can walk up and promote; the last floor always recruits a king.
- **Your recruit's orders.** You move your recruit. After your move or Wait shows on the board, green dots mark your recruit's legal squares (larger under a coin or powerup, so they show around it; a green ring for a monster it can capture, none on the turn it joins). Tap one to move it there, or tap the recruit, **Hold** (in Wait's place) or Space to keep it still; with the keyboard the cursor starts on the recruit, and arrows and Enter move it. Your move is already played by then, and Take-back undoes the whole turn. Your recruit may capture any monster, targets included (even the last floor's king), and that capture counts just like yours: the exit opens and your move count stops. Once a floor's challenge is complete your recruit moves itself for the rest of that room, capturing when it can and otherwise heading for the nearest monster: there is no order step, and an order a move still carries is ignored. On floors one and two it hunts for gold while you explore, and the challenge-complete message adds "Your recruit will automatically watch your back while you explore." On the last floor, the move that takes the king goes straight to the stairs. Off a Tandem floor, planted lines carry no orders: the recruit moves itself along them, never taking a target, and since you can always order the move it would make, every daily stays winnable. A Tandem floor's line gives the recruit the same orders you can.
- Castling: your rook and a king recruit (or, in practice, your king and a rook recruit), standing on one row with exactly two walkable, monster-free squares between them, trade places in one move: the king slides two squares toward the rook and the rook lands on the square the king crossed. Playing the rook, tap your king; playing a practice king, tap its landing square. As in chess, the king cannot castle out of, through, or into close range. Castling is also the recruit's move for that turn. Your first on a device shows a congratulation with an animated explainer; after that its line keeps a **What?** button and the journal has its page.
- Bishop pair: a bishop never leaves its square colour, and every recruit waits where your piece can reach it, so a bishop recruit would share your bishop's colour and only reach squares you already can. Instead, when your bishop recruits a bishop, the recruit's move that turn is a hop onto the other colour: one of the four side squares (up, down, left or right) of its square that is walkable and holds no monster. Those squares light up and you tap one (it can't hold). With all four blocked, every free square of the other colour lights up instead. A recruit moving itself, as on a planted line, takes the side square whose diagonals reach the most squares, or with all four blocked the nearest free one in king steps; ties go by the day's seed. The hop never captures, and like castling it is the recruit's move for that turn. It shows a **Holy duo!** callout, and its message has a **What?** button with an animated explainer: two bishops on opposite colours cover every square, which chess players call the bishop pair. It counts as a feat but adds nothing to the share card. The rule is the position: a bishop recruit standing on your bishop's colour always hops, so it happens again only if a Borrowed knight move puts your bishop on the recruit's colour. Floors one and two only, since the last floor's recruit is always a king.
- Tactics pay. **Fork 🔱**: end a move where your piece attacks two or more monsters at once (by the real capture rules: walls and other monsters block lines, knights jump) and they light up gold with a brief **Fork!** callout. Capture one of them on your very next move for +2 bonus gold. **Combo 🔥**: capture on moves in a row; a counter appears beside the exit door from the second capture, and the third capture in a row, and every one after it, pays +1 bonus gold. Waiting, a move without a capture, or a new floor ends a combo. The first fork you win and your first combo show a congratulation with an animated explainer. Tactics change only gold and feedback, never how anything moves or captures.
- Complete all three 8×8 rooms with unlimited moves.
- Room modifiers: each edition draws one modifier from its date, applied to all three floors and announced in a newspaper bulletin. About half the days are plain, a quarter Ice and a quarter moving terrain: a Crumbling floor or Gates, never both. 🧊 **Ice**: eight floor tiles per room are ice. Your rook, bishop or queen, or your pawn moving straight, that ends a non-capturing move on ice skids one more square the same way if that square is open and empty; the move preview draws an arrow to the landing square. Monsters and recruits never slide. 🕳️ **Crumbling floor**: about eight tiles per room are cracked. A cracked tile falls away when the piece standing on it steps off (yours, your recruit's or a monster's), and the pit it leaves blocks like a wall for the rest of the floor; knights jump it. Sliding across a cracked tile leaves it whole, a capture there holds it until the taker leaves, and a lunge at a square that has fallen lands nowhere. ⛓️ **Gates**: two or three squares per room are gates. Shut, a gate blocks like a wall; open, it is floor. Each is shut one move and open the next, changing once every piece has moved, so what is up now is down next move. A gate never shuts on a piece. Each room lays its tiles or gates around its planted line ([Generation and fairness](docs/generation.md)). A bishop's terrain day is always Gates. The dev server plays any date under either with `?composed=YYYY-MM-DD&modifier=cracks` or `&modifier=gates`.
- Each floor has a **par**: one move more than the planted line it was built around. Even par means you found the idea; under par, the exact line or a clever use of a powerup or your recruit. The status line shows `Par N` from the start of the floor, never a route, and the shop after the first or second floor heads with your result against par (`Floor 1 cleared · 2 over par · 3 gold`); the results paper lists every floor. Moves count as in the bot comparison: every move and wait on that floor until its challenge is complete, plus one per capture of your piece there (even after the challenge is complete) and one per paid hint. **Restart this floor** restores the gold and powers you arrived with and starts that floor's count over, so only the attempt that clears the floor is scored; paid hints stay paid and keep their strokes. Running out of legal moves starts the floor over the same way. Restarting today's dungeon starts a new try with fresh counts. Practice runs with a different piece, color or monsters have no par.
- **Treasure.** Most floors hold a chest, off the planted line (a Tandem floor never does). End a move of your own piece on its square while the door is still shut to take it: +3 gold, and a pixel diamond rises out of the chest as your move lands, hangs a moment and fades (it simply stands there under reduced-motion settings). From then on a small 💎 sits beside the status line's par, whatever your score. Clear the floor and a **Treasure!** stamp says so: that floor keeps the 💎 beside its par and on the share card, even over par. The chest always costs the spare move: some line takes it and still makes par, but no line that beats par passes through it, so each floor asks you to choose between under par and even with treasure. Your recruit, the monsters and the coin magnet never take it, and it is gone once the floor's challenge is complete. **Restart this floor** puts it back and takes back its gold. Practice rooms hold no chest.
- The share card is spoiler-free and golf-style. Its title gives the edition's date (with the year only for an edition from a past year) and your starting piece as a black chess symbol, whatever your color; archive and practice cards add **ARCHIVE** or **PRACTICE** after the game's name. The next line lists each floor reached, two spaces apart: the floor's marker (🟥 ⬛ 👑), its moves against par, paid hints included (`E` for even, `·` without a par), a 🇫🇷, 🏰 or 🔱 if you took en passant, castled or won a fork on that floor, and a 💎 if you took its chest, whatever your score. A link to the game closes the card:
  ```
  Dungeon Chess Daily · Nov 7 ♝
  🟥 +2  ⬛ E 🇫🇷  👑 −12 🔱
  https://dungeonchess.ryanlmelvin.com/
  ```
- There are no lives. Being captured adds one move to that floor's score, like a paid hint, unless a bone ward absorbs it, and play goes on from where you stand. A promoted pawn that is captured becomes a pawn again, returning to a safe square if needed. A piece with no legal move at all, even with every monster gone, starts its floor over, as **Restart this floor** does: the gold and powers you arrived with, and the floor's move count afresh. Nothing sends you back to floor one but your own **Restart today's dungeon**. A held Take-back can still undo the move that cornered you.

After you win the daily run with the official piece, the result paper shows the **Dungeon Bot**: your moves per floor and in total, beside the planted lines'. **Watch the bot's run** replays those lines on the board at a readable pace, with pause, step, restart, and speed controls; on a Tandem floor it shows the recruit joining and making its ordered moves. The bot's lines never take a chest. Practice wins never show it, so it can't spoil a daily run still in progress. The in-game status line counts your moves on the current floor since it began or was last restarted, the same count its par is measured against, and stops (marked ✓) once the floor's challenge is complete, though a capture still adds one; practice runs show the same floor count without a par.

**Retry floor** plays a floor you have already cleared again, free, once its result is locked in. The shop's last row has it beside **Descend**, for the floor just cleared; on the results paper the bot table's **F1**, **F2** and **F3** headings are buttons under a **Retry** label. A retry opens the floor as you first met it, with the powers you arrived with, on the real board, and it is a sandbox. Its par chip is filled gold and reads `Retry · Par 4`, so it can't pass for the run's own; gold is put away, since nothing is for sale. A bar under the board (in the right margin on a wide screen) has **Undo**, which steps back one whole turn (your move, your recruit's order and every monster's answer), free and as often as you like, even past a capture; **Reset**, the floor's start again with the count at zero, as **Restart this floor** does in the real run; and **Done**, which returns to the shop or the results paper as you left it. U and R do the first two on a keyboard. Moves count against par as usual, and when the floor is cleared the chip gives the result (`Retry · 1 under par`) and play stops: a retry has no stairs, shop, paid hints, cracking ceiling or Take-back, and no explainer opens by itself in it. Nothing in a retry touches the real run: no save, score, gold, powers, streak, journal page, share card, archive mark or backup changes, the retry itself is never saved, and reloading or opening another screen discards it and brings the run back where it stood. The results paper opens floor 1 and floor 3 exactly as the run met them, and floor 2 too while the app has stayed open since you played it; after a reload floor 2 opens as its room alone, without the powers you brought there, because a save keeps only the arrival of the floor it is on. Official runs only, daily and archive; practice already restarts freely. The sandbox is `src/retry.js`.

Everyone gets the same UTC-dated edition. After winning, choose any of the six pieces in white or black for unlimited practice. **Monsters** plays the same rooms at any tier, Gentle to Fiendish, starting with the day's own, Fair. The rooms are the official challenge's, fitted to the piece you choose: coins, power-ups and the waiting recruit it couldn't reach move to squares it can, and walls that box it in at the start are cleared (a pawn plays the room as it is). No practice room holds a chest, even with the day's own piece. Alternate pieces and monsters are not checked for solvability and may be unable to win. Only runs using the official starting piece, color and monsters offer emoji sharing, including labeled practice replays. Pawn promotion does not change sharing eligibility. Practice saves separately from the daily run and resumes after reloading; use **Return to daily challenge** in the menu to revisit official progress or results. Emoji sharing reveals your result without exposing the newspaper clues, monster movement roles, or route.

Open **Archive** in the menu to play any edition you missed. It lists every past edition by date, back to the first, with this device's result for each: won, lost, in progress, or not played. A past edition is the same certified dungeon everyone got that day and keeps its own save, practice runs included. Its shared result is labeled **ARCHIVE** so it never passes for today's. Use **Back to today** to return to the current edition.

**Stats**, in the menu, counts the editions you entered and won on their own UTC day with the official piece: played, won, your current and best streak, how your floors came out against par (under, even, +1, +2, +3 or more) and wins by piece. Past editions played from the archive and practice runs never count. A streak is days won in a row; today's edition doesn't break it until the day is over. From two days it shows beside the paper's edition number (`🔥12`) and closes the title line of that day's share card, never an **ARCHIVE** or **PRACTICE** card: `Dungeon Chess Daily · Nov 7 ♝ · 🔥12-day streak`. Nothing announces a streak or its end. The record behind it, the ledger (`src/stats.js`), is kept apart from the saves and from the rules version, so rule changes and rebuilt editions leave it alone, and the first scored win of a day stands. It began from the saves already on the device, counting each official save as played on its own day (a win under an older ruleset counts without a score against par). Backups carry it, and a restore merges it as a union of days; a backup from before the ledger has its saves counted the same way.

**Journal**, in the menu, is a book of lesson pages: one for each idea a floor is built around (bait, squeeze, tandem, the mating net, the royal pair, a pawn's tempo and crown lines, and the two rare ones, the Standstill and the Lure) and one for each special move (fork, combo, en passant, castling, the bishop pair). Each page has a name, a line or two and an animated diagram; turn pages with ‹ ›, the arrow keys or a swipe. You find a floor's idea by clearing that floor at par or better, and a special move by playing it, with the official piece only (daily or archive, never practice or other monsters). A page remembers which of the five daily pieces you found it with. Pages not found yet show dark under a question mark, with the pieces that can find them, so the book hints at what is left; a floor that fell back to a plain walk has no idea and no page. When a find is new (a new page, or a known page with a new piece), the shop between floors, or the results paper after the last floor, shows the page small over its heading for about three seconds, then it flies into a book beside a one-line note (`New page: Bait`). It takes no tap, opens no dialog and delays nothing: descend at once and it is gone. With reduced motion only the note shows. A find that isn't new shows just the idea's name there; tap the line to open the journal on that page. On a short phone the results paper has no line to spare, so the note takes the summary line's place. The journal is one record on the device (`journal`), apart from the saves and the rules version; backups carry it and a restore merges finds. It starts from the runs already saved on the device, filed once without any of this.

The mobile game gives the board the screen. Open the in-game menu for the paper, instructions, sound, music, saved progress, or a downloaded update. On a brand-new device (nothing saved yet), a three-page **Quick start** opens by itself the first time you enter a dungeon: the goal, monsters in disguise, and danger. **Play** (or ✕) starts the game; everything else the game explains as it comes up. It never opens on its own again, and devices with existing saves never see it. The seen flag stays out of backups. **How to play** in the menu is the full manual: the Quick start first, then short pages on every rule, each sized to fit a phone screen (down to an iPhone SE in Safari) without scrolling.

## Install and offline use

Use **Install** from the newspaper or result screen. The installation dialog includes instructions for Safari on iPhone/iPad, Chrome on Android, and desktop browsers. After its first complete download, the game and future deterministic daily editions work offline. Three.js, sprites, and other runtime assets are bundled locally.

Progress saves automatically on the device. **Download backup** and **Restore backup** in the menu transfer saved editions between browsers or installed apps; there is no account or automatic cloud synchronization. Before moving to an installed app, download a backup in the browser and restore it in the app. Restore merges saved days: won runs take priority, playable runs take priority over failed attempts, unfinished runs keep the later room, and ties keep the current run. Existing sound preferences stay unchanged. **Enable persistent storage** asks the browser to protect local data from automatic cleanup. The browser may grant or decline the request, and clearing browser data can still erase progress, so keep a backup. Certified editions use version 13 save keys (the chest is a new item that pays gold). The streak ledger and the journal are kept apart from the saves, so the change leaves them as they were. Earlier ruleset runs remain archived and included in backups; their old layouts are not loaded into the new certified editions.

Updates download in the background. **Update available**, including in the game menu, saves progress before reloading and refuses activation if saving fails. Other open windows keep their own reload choice. [Update recovery](https://dungeonchess.ryanlmelvin.com/update.html) works independently of the main app and preserves stored progress. Service-worker caches and saves are scoped to this project's path.

## Develop

Requires Node.js 22.12 or newer.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
node scripts/serve.mjs
```

The production preview serves `dist/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4320/dungeon-chess-daily/`, matching the GitHub Pages project prefix. Use `HOST` and `PORT` environment variables to change its address. Development mode does not register the production service worker.

`src/game.js` exposes the certified game API. `src/rules.js` contains the shared gameplay simulator; `src/tactics.js` scores forks and combos (bonus gold and feats only) and `src/tactics-art.js` draws their explainers; `src/journal.js` is the journal's pages and bookkeeping, `src/journal-art.js` its diagrams and `src/journal-ui.js` (with `src/journal.css`) its dialog, cue and moment; `src/composer.js` composes each edition's floors at build time, and `src/generation.js` loads the stored editions and, with `src/fairness.js`, replays their certificates. `src/renderer.js` renders the pixelated Three.js board. `src/sprites.js` contains original monster and chess-piece pixel art. `src/main.js` owns input, the newspaper, local progress, and sharing. `src/retry.js` is the Retry floor sandbox (a cleared floor's start, its Undo and Reset), which reads no storage and never changes the run it was opened from. A move or wait may carry a recruit order (`recruit: { x, y }` or `'hold'`) as every turn a player takes does; without one the recruit's own AI moves it, as on a certified route, which carries orders on a Tandem floor only. `recruitChoice` in `src/rules.js` previews the recruit's legal squares for that order without changing anything (no captures on the move it joins; on the bishop pair's hop, the squares of the other colour it may hop to, flagged `hop`; on a cleared floor one or two, none: the recruit moves itself there).

The build fingerprints shipped content and generates an integrity-verified offline cache. `dist/` is generated and is not committed. Run the engine and PWA tests before publishing. Browser checks should cover a complete run, touch input, save/resume, newspaper hints, sharing, installation, offline reopening, and updating an older installed release. Physical installed-iPhone behavior requires device testing.

## Generation and fairness

Every edition is composed, not searched. `src/composer.js` builds each floor around a short planted line (three or four moves), places what its idea needs, then dresses the room: decoys that roam (on floor 1 they sleep through the line, then wake), coins, a powerup and a recruit, off the line. It keeps a floor only when an exact search over every line of play finds no shorter win and the line uses its idea, a hasty player who takes a guarded target and absorbs the capture can't win in two, and forty runs of a bot that plays like a person show neither a two-move shortcut nor a slog. Monsters break ties by a seed, so a floor is a one-player puzzle, the same everywhere. Three things ride on that, all part of every stored edition: floor 2's subtle forms, kept only when no win within par skips the idea, a neutral first move costs par and the bot seldom makes par (`src/tight.js`); a Tandem on floor 1 or 2 of a day whose matchup has one, whose line orders the floor's recruit; and a chest off each floor's line, laid only where a proved win within par takes it, no win of the line's own length does, and the bot seldom takes it by accident (`src/treasure.js`). The planted lines are the day's certificate, replayed through the production engine as one run (`verifyWinningRoute` in `src/fairness.js`), the recruit on orders on a Tandem floor alone.

Every build composes each edition from launch through two years past the build date and ships its rooms (chests included), its lines (with a Tandem floor's recruit orders), each floor's idea and the day's Tandem floor, if it has one, in `src/editions.js` (`npm run build:editions`; incremental, so it only composes new or stale dates, about a second a day at the median; `-- --full` composes them all, about five minutes on seven threads). The player's app never carries the composer: opening a day decodes its stored rooms and replays the lines once more before play. A date past the window borrows a stored day of the same piece and modifier, chosen by the date, played under its own names and colors. `npm run verify:editions` replays every stored edition, `npm run audit:editions` composes them all again and compares, and `npm run audit:dungeons -- 2026-11-01 365` replays a year of dailies, checking the save after every move. After any change to the rules, monster AI, room modifiers, the composer or the human-like bot, run `npm run build:editions -- --full` and `npm test`. [Generation and fairness](docs/generation.md) explains the guarantee, the composer's checks, par and versioning; [Puzzle-first idea](docs/puzzle-first-idea.md) records how composed floors came about and what was measured. `node scripts/measure-composer.mjs` measures the composer over a range of dates, and `node scripts/measure-human-par.mjs` probes the stored editions with the human-like bot; `measure-tight.mjs` and `measure-treasure.mjs` measure the tight forms and the chests. The measuring scripts compose what the build does; `--no-treasure`, `--no-partner` and `--no-tight` each turn one option off to compare.

## Publish

Push to `main`. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` installs dependencies, runs tests, builds, and deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages. Set the repository's Pages source to **GitHub Actions**. Asset URLs, manifest scope, and worker registration use relative paths. After deployment, verify the live `release.json` and play the published edition.
