# Dungeon Chess Daily

[Play Dungeon Chess Daily](https://ryanlmelvin.github.io/dungeon-chess-daily/) · [Source](https://github.com/ryanlmelvin/dungeon-chess-daily)

A short daily dungeon played with chess moves. A twirling pixel newspaper hints at three disguised targets through colorful tabloid headlines. Take one chess piece through three crowded rooms, identify your targets, collect powerups, and share a spoiler-free emoji result. Designed for a complete daily run in five minutes or less.

By [Ryan L. Melvin](https://ryanlmelvin.com).

## Play

- Your daily piece is selected from pawn, knight, bishop, rook, and queen with equal probability. The king is never the daily piece: it can't step next to the monster king and its recruit can't capture it, so the last floor could be unwinnable. You can still choose a king for practice, with a warning. It is always white, so you move first; black is a practice choice. It moves and captures like that chess piece. Tap your piece to see legal destinations, then tap a destination to move.
- Capture a monster by landing on its square. A pawn advances into empty squares and captures diagonally; reaching the far rank promotes it to a queen. En passant works both ways: a monster pawn that double-steps past your pawn can be captured on your very next move as if it had moved one square, and a monster pawn captures yours the same way when you double-step past it, landing on the square your pawn skipped. Taking one shows a congratulation with an animated explainer; being taken offers a **What?** button with the same explainer.
- Each edition draws nine monster species from twenty-six original sprites, with six randomized color palettes. Each floor mixes up to three species: its targets and, depending on the day, up to four decoys. Their chess roles stay hidden behind those appearances; read the newspaper's three clues and watch their moves.
- Monsters move like their chess role. Knights hover a jump away, hunting squares that strike you or cover your escape squares, and rarely land where you can take them. Rooks, bishops and queens slide onto open lines that cut off your escape squares rather than just closing in. Pawns march in chains, stepping where another pawn guards them. Any monster you threaten without a guard retreats beside a defender if it can. Habits are preferences, not perfect play, and a tie between equal steps is settled by the day's seed.
- Difficulty rises through the week: Monday is **Gentle**, Tuesday **Fair**, Wednesday **Tricky**, Thursday and Friday **Tough**, and the weekend **Fiendish**. The newspaper names the day's tier (for example "THURSDAY · TOUGH") and nothing else about the dungeon. Each tier is calibrated per piece, so a queen day and a pawn day of the same tier aim for the same measured difficulty: stronger pieces meet more guards, scattered targets, and more decoys.
- Pillars, crates, barrels, and rocks fill each floor. They block movement and attack lanes; knights jump over them. Layouts preserve movement connectivity and pawn promotion lanes. Every edition also has a replay-verified winning route through combat within fifty moves, without powerups or companions (except the final floor's own recruit) and without lost lives, except that a pawn's route may spend up to four of its eight; pawn promotion and waiting are allowed.
- White acts first each turn. When you are white, your move resolves before the monsters respond; when you are black, the monsters act before your selected move. If they block that destination or a hit returns you to a pawn, choose again without another enemy response. A threatened capture winds up before it lands, showing its full attack path. Ending your move next to a monster that attacks your square, or on a square a knight attacks, is captured at once with no warning, including when you take a monster another one guards. Queens, rooks, and bishops give warning instead: step into their line from farther away and they close in one square and wind up. As in chess, a king (a practice choice) cannot move into check, so squares a nearby monster attacks are not offered. **Wait** (or Space) skips your move so monsters come to you; one that walks into range only winds up, so you strike first.
- Floors one and two each ask you to capture all enemies of a particular chess role; the third always asks for the king. Every target group has the exact starting chess-army count: eight matching pawns, two matching rooks, knights, or bishops, or one queen or king. Other roles never exceed those limits. Capturing a different role earns gold but does not complete the challenge. The exit is a door in the back wall, behind the top-center square; it stays shut until every target is captured, then opens onto the stairs. Tap the stairs, or the exit button from anywhere, to leave. The square in front of the door stays an ordinary square, so a pawn can still promote there.
- Coins, optional captures, and tactic bonuses earn gold to spend on powerups in the shop between floors. Every floor holds a recruit that moves after you for that floor, and its captures count, but it can never capture that floor's challenge piece. Floors one and two may recruit any piece; the last floor recruits a rook, queen, or king.
- Castling: your rook and a king recruit (or, in practice, your king and a rook recruit), standing on one row with exactly two walkable, monster-free squares between them, trade places in one move: the king slides two squares toward the rook and the rook lands on the square the king crossed. Playing the rook, tap your king; playing a practice king, tap its landing square. As in chess, the king cannot castle out of, through, or into close range. Castling is also the recruit's move for that turn, and shows a congratulation with an animated explainer.
- Tactics pay. **Fork 🍴**: end a move where your piece attacks two or more monsters at once (by the real capture rules: walls and other monsters block lines, knights jump) and they light up gold with a brief **Fork!** callout. Capture one of them on your very next move for +2 bonus gold. **Combo 🔥**: capture on moves in a row; a counter appears from the second capture, and the third capture in a row, and every one after it, pays +1 bonus gold. Waiting, a move without a capture, or a new floor ends a combo. The first fork you win and your first combo show a congratulation with an animated explainer. Tactics change only gold and feedback, never how anything moves or captures.
- Complete all three 8×8 rooms with unlimited moves.
- Room modifiers: each edition draws one modifier from its date, applied to all three floors and announced in a newspaper bulletin. About one day in four is plain. 🌑 **Dark**: you see only squares within two of your piece, plus any monster winding up, its surroundings and its red path; monsters you've seen stay as faded ghosts where you last saw them. Only the drawing changes, never the rules. 🧊 **Ice**: eight floor tiles per room are ice. Your rook, bishop or queen, or your pawn moving straight, that ends a non-capturing move on ice skids one more square the same way if that square is open and empty; the move preview draws an arrow to the landing square. Monsters and recruits never slide. 🏚️ **Collapse**: every 10 moves on a floor, one more monster (one of the floor's decoy species, never a pawn or the target) enters on the square in front of the door once it is free, at most three per floor and none after the stairs open.
- Each floor has a **par**: the number of moves the certified winning route (the Dungeon Bot below) spent on it. The status line shows `Par N` from the start of the floor, never the route itself. Clearing a floor in at most par moves earns ⭐⭐⭐, within five over par ⭐⭐, and otherwise ⭐; a toast announces them and they are saved with the floor. Moves count as in the bot comparison: every move and wait on that floor in the current try. Practice runs with a different piece or color have no par and no stars.
- The share line starts with the day's modifier emoji (nothing on plain days), then shows a 💀 for each earlier try, then each floor reached as ❤️ (no life lost) or a 💔 per life lost, each followed by 🇫🇷 for every en passant and 🏰 for every castle you made on that floor, then 🍴 for every fork you won and 🔥 for every combo capture there, then 🏆 (escaped) or ⏳ (still playing), and your piece; a won run ends with a space and its total par stars out of nine, for example `🧊💀❤️🇫🇷🍴💔❤️🏰🔥🔥🏆♙ ⭐7`.
- Lives match the number of that piece in a chess army: eight pawns, two rooks, knights, or bishops, and one queen (or king, in practice). A hit costs one life unless a shield absorbs it. A promoted pawn keeps its remaining lives but becomes a pawn again after losing one, returning to a safe square if needed. Losing all lives restarts the same daily dungeon from floor one, resetting gold and powers.

After you win the daily run with the official piece, the result paper shows the **Dungeon Bot**: your moves per floor and in total, with your par stars per floor and out of nine, beside those of the route the fairness checker found when it certified the dungeon (no powerups, and no lost lives except on pawn days, where it may spend up to four). **Watch the bot's run** replays that route on the board at a readable pace, with pause, step, restart, and speed controls. Practice wins never show it, so it can't spoil a daily run still in progress. The in-game status line counts your moves on the current try.

Everyone gets the same UTC-dated edition. After winning, losing all lives, or running out of legal moves, choose any of the six pieces in white or black for unlimited practice. The rooms stay identical to the official challenge; alternate pieces are not checked for solvability and may be unable to win. Only runs using the official starting piece and color offer emoji sharing, including labeled practice replays. Pawn promotion does not change sharing eligibility. Practice saves separately from the daily run and resumes after reloading; use **Return to daily challenge** in the menu to revisit official progress or results. Emoji sharing reveals your result without exposing the newspaper clues, monster movement roles, or route.

Open **Archive** in the menu to play any edition you missed. It lists every past edition back to No. 001 with this device's result for each: won, lost, in progress, or not played. A past edition is the same certified dungeon everyone got that day and keeps its own save, practice runs included. Its shared result is labeled **ARCHIVE** so it never passes for today's. Use **Back to today** to return to the current edition.

The mobile game gives the board the screen. Open the in-game menu for the paper, instructions, sound, music, saved progress, or a downloaded update.

## Install and offline use

Use **Install** from the newspaper or result screen. The installation dialog includes instructions for Safari on iPhone/iPad, Chrome on Android, and desktop browsers. After its first complete download, the game and future deterministic daily editions work offline. Three.js, sprites, and other runtime assets are bundled locally.

Progress saves automatically on the device. **Download backup** and **Restore backup** in the menu transfer saved editions between browsers or installed apps; there is no account or automatic cloud synchronization. Before moving to an installed app, download a backup in the browser and restore it in the app. Restore merges saved days: won runs take priority, playable runs take priority over failed attempts, unfinished runs keep the later room, and ties keep the current run. Existing sound preferences stay unchanged. **Enable persistent storage** asks the browser to protect local data from automatic cleanup. The browser may grant or decline the request, and clearing browser data can still erase progress, so keep a backup. Certified editions use version 6 save keys. Earlier ruleset runs remain archived and included in backups; their old layouts are not loaded into the new certified editions.

Updates download in the background. **Update available**, including in the game menu, saves progress before reloading and refuses activation if saving fails. Other open windows keep their own reload choice. [Update recovery](https://ryanlmelvin.github.io/dungeon-chess-daily/update.html) works independently of the main app and preserves stored progress. Service-worker caches and saves are scoped to this project's path.

## Develop

Requires Node.js 22.12 or newer.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
node scripts/serve.mjs
```

The production preview serves `dist/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4320/dungeon-chess-daily/`, matching the GitHub Pages project prefix. Use `HOST` and `PORT` environment variables to change its address. Development mode does not register the production service worker.

`src/game.js` exposes the certified game API. `src/rules.js` contains the shared gameplay simulator; `src/tactics.js` scores forks and combos (bonus gold and feats only) and `src/tactics-art.js` draws their explainers; `src/generation.js` and `src/fairness.js` generate and replay-certify editions. `src/renderer.js` renders the pixelated Three.js board. `src/sprites.js` contains original monster and chess-piece pixel art. `src/main.js` owns input, the newspaper, local progress, and sharing.

The build fingerprints shipped content and generates an integrity-verified offline cache. `dist/` is generated and is not committed. Run the engine and PWA tests before publishing. Browser checks should cover a complete run, touch input, save/resume, newspaper hints, sharing, installation, offline reopening, and updating an older installed release. Physical installed-iPhone behavior requires device testing.

## Generation and fairness

Generation uses fixed structural constraints, at most two deterministic candidate searches, and a replay-verified fallback bank. It never runs an open-ended repair loop or treats an unfinished search as proof. The search uses fixed work budgets, so different device speeds cannot change the daily edition. Every stationary pickup also passes a piece-specific terrain reachability check on every floor, including bishop color restrictions and pawn promotion; knights may revisit squares. Difficulty tiers vary obstacles, decoys, guards, target placement, and measured attack-lane exposure separately from the no-powerup victory requirement.

Every build certifies each edition from launch through two years past the build date and ships the results in `src/editions.js` (`npm run build:editions`; incremental, so it only searches new or stale dates), so opening a day replays a stored route in milliseconds instead of searching. A date past that window searches once on the device, then the result is remembered in local storage. Run `npm run verify:fallbacks` to replay all 75 tier/piece/modifier combinations (plain, ice and collapse; dark plays by plain rules and shares plain certificates), or `npm run audit:dungeons -- 2026-11-05 365` to audit a year of daily editions. After any change to rules, monster AI, room modifiers, or search, re-run `npm run calibrate:difficulty` and then `node scripts/build-fallbacks.mjs`. [Generation and fairness](docs/generation.md) explains the guarantee, the difficulty score and tiers, calibration, and versioning requirements. The score measures a solver's route, not proven human difficulty.

## Publish

Push to `main`. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` installs dependencies, runs tests, builds, and deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages. Set the repository's Pages source to **GitHub Actions**. Asset URLs, manifest scope, and worker registration use relative paths. After deployment, verify the live `release.json` and play the published edition.
